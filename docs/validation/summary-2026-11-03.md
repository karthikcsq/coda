# Meeting summaries that lead with takeaways — build 21

## Problem

The previous instruction optimized for coverage: detailed notes in first-discussed
order, then decisions and actions. Notes were accurate and complete, but every topic
had equal weight. On the designated 20-minute meeting, the meeting's purpose and its
main outcome appeared only in the last of 12 notes.

## What practitioners and research recommend

- Bottom line up front (BLUF / TL;DR): purpose and outcome before any detail.
- Outcome-first ordering: decisions, actions, then risks and open questions, then context.
- Separate quick highlights from detailed minutes. Microsoft's LLM recap study
  (Asthana et al., CSCW 2024, arXiv 2307.15793) found users want both, for different
  jobs; it is a seven-person qualitative study, so treat it as design guidance.
- Explicit open questions; grounding every claim in source excerpts for verification.

## Format

`brief` (one or two sentences: purpose and bottom line), `takeaways` (3–5, ranked),
`decisions`, `actions`, `openQuestions`, then topic `notes` (a 2–5 word heading plus
detail). Notes remain stored under `overview`; summaries saved before this change load
unchanged, show their notes under the old heading, and offer regeneration. Owners and
deadlines are kept only on actions and headings only on notes, whatever the model returns.
Every item, including the brief, must cite known segment IDs.

## Prompt iteration

Three variants were run with the configured model against two saved meetings: a
20-minute two-person preparation call for a public event, and a 7-minute informal
multi-speaker chat with no agenda. Transcripts and outputs stayed in a scratch folder
outside the repository.

- A — BLUF layout only. The brief named the occasion generically; takeaways were
  mostly ranked background rather than what to do next.
- B — adds "work out why the meeting happened and what participants will do next".
  The brief named the actual event and format. On the informal chat it said no shared
  decision emerged, but still listed a personal choice as a decision and turned
  "stop the recording" into an action.
- C — B plus: preparation meetings capture what to cover, emphasize or avoid and the
  format; answers to planning questions count even when the answer is "nothing";
  recording logistics are ignored; personal choices are not group decisions.
  C caught the event-prep outcome the earlier variants missed, and produced no invented
  decisions or actions on the informal chat. It also flagged a disputed product claim
  as unsettled instead of stating it.
- Shipped: C plus a rule against evaluative language ("strongest"), which C
  occasionally added. Prompt examples are generic and do not come from the test meetings.

This is a qualitative comparison on two meetings, not a benchmark. The planning-question
guidance was written after seeing the first meeting, so that meeting is not a blind test.
